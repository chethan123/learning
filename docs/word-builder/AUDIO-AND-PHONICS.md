# Word Builder: audio and phonics research (Nov 2026)

Research notes for the next restructure of Word Builder. They cover four
questions:

1. Can a better voice (ElevenLabs, Gemini, a human recording) replace the
   Kokoro clips?
2. Can the game colour-code the sounds being spoken?
3. Can there be a grown-ups tool for recording and re-recording audio?
4. Should audio stay bundled, or become editable on the live site?

**Nothing here has been built.** Section 6 lists the decisions still open.

The research ran on 12–13 Nov 2026. Vendor models, prices and terms change
quickly, so re-check sections 2.2–2.4 before acting on them. Claims marked
**(verified)** were checked directly against the primary source. The other
claims come from research agents' reading of the cited pages.

Related: [`PLAN.md`](./PLAN.md) holds the decisions this would change: D7
(audio is bundled clips), D8 (Kokoro voice) and D14 (sound out one sound at a
time).

## 1. Where things stand

- **Clips:** 157 word clips and 28 sound clips
  (`a b ch d e f g h i j k l m n ng o p r s sh t th u v w x y z`). They live in
  `src/pages/word-builder/assets/audio/{words,sounds}/` and are generated by
  `scripts/word-builder/generate-audio.py`, which uses Kokoro-82M and voice
  `af_heart`.
- **How the URLs are built:** `assets.ts` builds clip URLs at build time with
  `import.meta.glob`, so changing a clip means rebuilding and redeploying.
  `generate-audio.py` skips files that already exist, so a hand-made MP3 with
  the same name is never overwritten.
- **The Kokoro workaround:** Kokoro can't say a bare phoneme. Each sound is
  synthesised inside a carrier syllable (`bɑ`, `ʌs`) and cut out with signal
  heuristics. PR #2 lists about half the sounds as doubtful
  (`b d g f th h z v n ng l r`), plus about 20 word clips. Nobody has listened
  to them all.
- **Sounding out:** `ui/play.ts` plays one sound clip per grapheme, in reading
  order, while that tile half or card pulses (`is-sounding`). Then it plays the
  word. Nothing is colour-coded.
- **The grown-ups page:** `#/grown-ups` (`ui/grown-ups.ts`) already shows
  every round with a play button per word and per sound. That is the natural
  home for a recording tool.
- **Graphemes:** `content/letter-sets.ts` models single letters plus the
  digraphs `ll ss ck sh th ch ng`. A blend tile (`fr`) is two graphemes.
  `soundId()` shares clips: `c`/`ck` → `k`, `ll` → `l`, `ss` → `s`.

## 2. Voice: replacing or improving the Kokoro clips

### 2.1 Headline findings

1. **ElevenLabs and the Gemini API both prohibit children's products.**
   - ElevenLabs Use Policy, "Use by and for Minors", last updated 9 Oct 2026
     **(verified)**: "Apps, products, or experiences directed at individuals
     under 18 are prohibited unless we have assessed and approved them in
     writing." Approved apps must also get the parental consent the law
     requires. <https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy>
   - Gemini API Additional Terms **(verified in several published
     versions)**: you may not use the Services "as part of a website,
     application, or other service … that is directed towards or is likely to
     be accessed by individuals under the age of 18". Users must also be 18 or
     older. <https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms>,
     <https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms-archive/terms_11_20_25>. A Google
     staff reply (not a terms document) says this applies on Vertex AI too.
   - Unclear: whether clips generated once and served as static files count
     as using the service "as part of" the site. Read literally, a
     kindergarten game is what these clauses describe. Don't use either one
     without written approval or legal advice.
2. **No hosted TTS reliably produces a pure isolated phoneme.** Phoneme tags
   and IPA input control how a word is pronounced; they don't produce a bare,
   stretchable sound.
   - The Open Letter Sounds project
     (<https://github.com/tavinathanson/nemophones>) puts it this way: TTS
     engines "speak whatever text they are given. A pure sound has no spelling
     they can read." Typing `b` gives "bee"; typing `buh` keeps the vowel.
   - That project switched to an audio-native model (OpenAI `gpt-audio-1.5`),
     generated 20–40 takes per sound and filtered them. For `/b/`, 9 of 40
     passed.
3. **Cost doesn't decide anything.** Generating every clip five times costs
   under $10 on any service.

### 2.2 ElevenLabs

- **Models:** `eleven_v4`, `eleven_v4_turbo` (launched 28 Sep 2026),
  `eleven_v3`, `eleven_multilingual_v2`, `eleven_flash_v2_5` and
  `eleven_flash_v2`. Turbo v2/v2.5 are deprecated.
  <https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models>
- **Phoneme control:**
  - SSML `<phoneme>` (CMU Arpabet or IPA) works only on `eleven_flash_v2`,
    and only for individual words.
  - v4 accepts IPA written between slashes, best effort ("can still vary by
    voice and phrase").
  - v3 has no phoneme or `<break>` tags, only audio tags such as
    `[whispering]`.
  - Pronunciation dictionaries (`.pls`): up to 3 per request, and their
    phoneme rules apply only on models that support phonemes.
  - Docs: <https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/controls>
- **Isolated sounds:** no documentation or educator report shows a clean
  `/b/`. Spellings like "mmm" and "sss" may work for continuous sounds. For
  stops (`b d g p t k`), nothing found suggests they work.
- **Timing data:**
  - The TTS `/with-timestamps` endpoint gives per-character start and end
    times.
  - The Forced Alignment API ("Cue", Oct 2026) gives characters and words.
    Its errors were benchmarked at 57–179 ms, at word level.
  - Characters aren't phonemes: `sh` gets two timings. Errors of that size
    are large in a 0.5 s word.
- **Voices:**
  - Voice Design (a voice made from a text prompt) is on all plans.
  - Instant Voice Cloning needs Starter or above and under 2 minutes of
    audio. Cloning an adult parent is allowed. Recordings of under-18s may not
    be used to make a voice.
  - Child-like voices are banned from the Voice Library.
- **Pricing:**
  - Free tier: 10k credits a month, non-commercial, and published titles must
    include "elevenlabs.io".
  - Starter: $6 a month with a commercial licence.
  - One full set is about 810 characters, so 5 takes is about 4k — within one
    Starter month.
  - <https://elevenlabs.io/pricing>
- **Licence:** you keep the rights to output from a paid plan.
  - The "no standalone audio or sample packs" rule an earlier note mentioned
    is in the **Music** terms, not the speech terms.
  - The use policy restricts handing output to third parties on terms more
    permissive than ElevenLabs' own. So don't put generated clips under
    CC0/CC-BY in a public repository.
- **API:** `POST /v1/text-to-speech/{voice_id}?output_format=mp3_44100_128`
  with the `xi-api-key` header. `seed` is best effort, not deterministic. A
  build-time script with the key in an environment variable is the normal
  pattern; the key never reaches the browser.

### 2.3 Gemini TTS

- **Models:**
  - Gemini API / AI Studio: `gemini-3.8-flash-tts`, `-flash-lite-tts`,
    `gemini-3.1-flash-tts-preview`, `gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts`,
    `gemini-2.5-pro-preview-tts`.
  - Cloud TTS offers "Gemini-TTS" (2.5 and 3.1 variants).
  - <https://aistudio.google.com/docs/speech-generation>,
    <https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts>
- **Style control:** natural-language prompts ("slowly and clearly, like a
  kindergarten teacher") and inline tags such as `<short pause>`. Very natural
  sounding.
- **Not documented:** IPA, phoneme or SSML input for Gemini-TTS. Chirp 3 HD,
  a different Cloud TTS voice family, does accept IPA/X-SAMPA custom
  pronunciations.
- **Isolated sounds:** untested. As an audio language model, a style prompt
  might work the way it did for Open Letter Sounds.
- **Output:** WAV/PCM at 24 kHz mono; Cloud TTS can output MP3. No timestamps
  and no seed are documented. All output carries a SynthID watermark.
- **Cost:** about $0.30 for the whole job. Free-tier data may be used to
  improve Google products.
- **Terms:** see the under-18 clause in 2.1. Google doesn't claim ownership of
  the output.

### 2.5 A human recording (recommended)

A parent records all 185 items: 28 sounds and 157 words. Add 157 stretched
takes if smooth blending is adopted (section 3).

- **Why:**
  - It's the only option that gives correct, schwa-free stop sounds.
  - It has no terms or licence restrictions.
  - A familiar voice helps the child.
  - The same voice for "c… a… t" and "cat" shows how the sounds merge into
    the word. Human sounds with TTS words would break that.
- **Effort:** 185 items × 2–3 takes is about 20–30 minutes at the mic, plus
  1–2 hours of trimming and review. A studio tool (section 4) would do most
  of the trimming and review.
- **Equipment:**
  - A recent phone recording uncompressed audio is enough; a $50–100 USB
    cardioid mic is better.
  - Record in a closet full of clothes or under a duvet, 15–20 cm from the
    mic and slightly off-axis so `b` and `p` don't pop.
  - 48 kHz / 24-bit.
- **Processing:**
  - Split on silence (ffmpeg `silencedetect`/`silenceremove`) and trim to
    about 30 ms of padding.
  - Level the volume with `loudnorm` (EBU R128, two-pass). Integrated
    loudness is unreliable on clips under a second, so check the result or
    use RMS/peak levelling for the sounds.
  - Encode to match the current clips (mono MP3).
- **Consistency:** record in one session with a fixed mic distance and gain.
  In a later session, re-record a few anchor words and match the levels.
- **How to say the sounds** (Lancashire phonics booklet, Monster Phonics,
  Twinkl):
  - Stretch continuous sounds (`m s f v z n l r sh th ng`) for 0.6–1 s.
  - Clip stops (`b d g p t k ch j`) with no vowel after: start the word and
    stop.
  - `/h/` is just breath; keep unvoiced sounds unvoiced.
  - Short vowels last 0.3–0.4 s.
- **Fallbacks:**
  - Record only the 28 sounds and keep the Kokoro words. Less work, but the
    voice changes between the sounds and the word.
  - Record the sounds, then clone the parent's voice for the words with
    Chatterbox (local, no terms issue). Quality on single words is untested.
  - Azure or Polly for the words, once their terms are checked for children's
    products.

## 3. Phonics: colour-coding and showing letter groups

### 3.1 Evidence

- **Colour-coding the letters themselves has weak evidence, plus a known
  risk:**
  - The studies are mostly from the 1960s–80s.
  - Children latch onto the colour cue and struggle to transfer to black
    print. The 1976 _Journal of Learning Disabilities_ study recommends fading
    the colour out (<https://doi.org/10.1177/002221947600900912>).
  - Moats (1998) notes that beginners fix on incidental cues such as colour.
  - Donnelly et al. 2020 (RCT, ages 8–12) used blue vowels with picture cues.
    Decoding improved only while the cue was present; transfer was not
    tested. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7721157/>
- **Gattegno's _Words in Colour_ (1962):** a colour per phoneme, about 59 of
  them. The only evaluations found are 1960s exploratory studies; no modern
  trial.
- **Montessori moveable alphabet:** classically blue vowels and red/pink
  consonants, though some sets reverse it. The sources are practitioner
  material only.
- **What the main curricula do:** IES, UFLI and Letters and Sounds keep
  letters black and show grouping with boxes, dots and bars.
  - Letters and Sounds "sound buttons": a dot under each single-letter
    grapheme and a bar under a digraph.
  - Elkonin / sound boxes: one box per phoneme.
  - UFLI slides (lessons 43, 52): black letters, sound boxes.
  - Fundations teaches `-ng`/`-nk` as glued units; UFLI does not, as the
    `-nk` row in section 1 shows.
- **Smooth blending beats separated sounds:**
  - IES Recommendation 3 (strong evidence) says to "sound out smoothly…
    /mmmaaannn/ rather than /m/…/a/…/n/".
  - Gonzalez-Frey & Ehri 2021 (RCT, kindergartners): children taught
    connected phonation decoded new words better, including words starting
    with stop consonants, and the gain held at a delayed test. Children taught
    separated sounds more often forgot the first sound.
  - <https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/wwc_foundationalreading_040717.pdf>,
    <https://www.readingrockets.org/research-by-topic/connected-phonation-more-effective-segmented-phonation-teaching-beginning-readers>
- **Accessibility:**
  - WCAG 1.4.1 says colour can't be the only cue.
  - With the colour-blind-safe Okabe-Ito palette as _letter colours_ on
    white, only blue `#0072B2` reaches 4.5:1 contrast.
  - With dark text (`#1A1A1A`) on those colours as _tile tints_, everything
    except blue passes 4.5:1. So tint tiles; keep letters dark.

### 3.2 Design options

| Option                                                             | Strengths                                                                  | Risks                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| (a) A different colour per position                                | Shows the grouping                                                         | Colours mean nothing from word to word; visual noise                      |
| (b) Colour by category (vowel / consonant / digraph)               | Familiar from Montessori                                                   | Weak evidence; a "digraph colour" doesn't say which sound                 |
| (c) A fixed colour per phoneme (Words in Colour)                   | `c`/`ck` share a colour, which teaches the shared sound                    | 40+ colours can't be told apart; fails colour-blind checks; poor transfer |
| (d) No colour: dots and bars, plus a highlight synced to the audio | What curricula actually do                                                 | Least colourful                                                           |
| **(e) (d) plus one tint on the tile being spoken**                 | Colour only means "now speaking"; nothing depends on telling colours apart | Small                                                                     |

**Recommended: option (e).**

- Dark letters, one tile per grapheme, with a small gap between graphemes.
- A dot under each single letter; a bar under `ll ss ck sh th ch ng`.
- Blends stay two tiles with two dots.
- `-nk` is two tiles, with the `n` playing `/ŋ/`.
- `x` is one tile with a two-part highlight.
- The tile being spoken lifts and gets one high-contrast tint, such as
  Okabe-Ito orange behind dark text.
- If you want more colour, put it on backgrounds and rewards. Dots and bars
  can fade once a pattern is mastered.

### 3.3 Presentation sequence

1. **I do, separated:** each sound clip plays while its tile pulses and its
   dot or bar lights. The game does this today.
2. **I do, connected:** a stretched take ("mmmaaap") plays while a highlight
   sweeps across the tiles and they slide together. This is new and needs
   stretched recordings plus per-sound timing.
3. **Whole word:** the word plays at normal speed with every tile lit, then
   the picture appears.
4. **We do / you do (later rounds):** the child taps each tile to hear its
   sound, then taps once to hear the word after a pause to blend.

This would extend PLAN D14, which today says "sounded out one sound at a
time".

### 3.4 Timing inside a word (for step 2)

- **Storage:** one timing record per word, for example
  `{ word: "pink", segs: [{ g: "p", ph: "p", t0, t1 }, { g: "i", … }, { g: "n", ph: "ŋ", … }, { g: "k", … }] }`.
- **Playback:** start the clip with `AudioBufferSourceNode.start(when)`.
  Drive the highlight from `requestAnimationFrame`, comparing
  `ctx.currentTime - when` with `t0`. Turning a highlight on 30–50 ms early
  feels more in sync.
- **Getting the timings:** auto-align with the Montreal Forced Aligner (its
  phone tier gives phoneme intervals), then hand-check. Or tap-to-mark in the
  studio (section 4).
  - Aligners are trained on normal speech and will misplace boundaries in
    stretched takes, so a human check is required.
  - TTS timing outputs don't help: ElevenLabs gives per-character times, and
    Azure visemes merge `/n/` with `/t d θ/` and `/ŋ/` with `/k g/`.
- **Mapping letters to sounds:** for this word list, letters map almost
  one-to-one to sounds once `ng → ŋ`, n-before-k `→ ŋ`, `ck/ll/ss` as single
  sounds and `x → k+s` are encoded.

## 4. A recording studio for grown-ups

Wherever it lives, it would offer:

- **Checklist:** every sound, word and (optionally) stretched take, each
  marked as Kokoro, recorded or needs review, with a "not recorded yet"
  filter.
- **Prompt:** the item's picture plus how to say it ("stretch _mmm_ about a
  second", "clip _b_, no _uh_").
- **Keyboard flow:** **Space** records, **R** retries, **Enter** keeps, then it
  moves to the next item. Several takes per item, choose the best.
- **Mic setup:**
  - Device picker, level meter, warnings for clipping and room noise.
  - `getUserMedia` with `echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression` and
    `autoGainControl` set to `false`, so every take is processed the same way.
- **Capture:** raw PCM through an AudioWorklet. Safari's MediaRecorder gives
  AAC, which is lossy.
- **Processing:** auto-trim silence to about 30 ms of padding, a waveform with
  trim handles, volume levelled to match the other clips, and an A/B against
  the current clip.
- **Undo:** revert any item to its previous take or to Kokoro.
- **Timing marks:** tap as each sound plays back, or adjust suggested markers
  (section 3.4).
- **Validation:** the save path only accepts known clip ids (from the content
  manifest), so a request can never write to an arbitrary path.

Microphone access needs a secure context (`https` or `localhost`). The dev
server listens on a VM, so plain `http://<vm>:5173` won't get the mic. Use an
SSH tunnel (`ssh -L 5173:localhost:5173 …`), or record on the live site
behind TLS.

### 4.1 Where it lives

| Option                                     | How saving works                                                                                 | Trade-offs                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. Studio on the dev server                | A Vite dev middleware writes the MP3 into `assets/audio/`; commit, rebuild, deploy               | Git history is the undo; no production attack surface; needs the SSH tunnel; every fix needs a redeploy    |
| B. In-browser studio with export           | Takes stay in that browser (IndexedDB) and override clips on that device; export a zip to commit | Works on the live site with no server; fixes stay on one device; Safari may evict the storage              |
| **C. Studio on the live site (section 5)** | Uploaded to a small service and live on the next page load                                       | Fixes are immediate, and recording works straight from an iPad; adds a container, a login and stored state |

Option C was preferred in discussion because it makes audio editable without
a redeploy.

## 5. Editable audio: bundled defaults plus a live override layer

The aim is to correct or add pronunciations without a rebuild, while the
bundled clips stay as a safety net. This changes PLAN D7.

```text
browser ── https ──▶ Caddy (static, read-only root)
                     ├─ /                 game; bundled Kokoro clips are the defaults
                     ├─ /media/*          recordings from the volume (mounted read-only)
                     │    manifest.json   active clip (and timings) for each word or sound
                     └─ /api/*, /studio   ──(auth)──▶ small Node service ──▶ ./volumes/audio (rw)
```

- **Playback never depends on the new service:**
  - On start, the game loads `/media/manifest.json` (a few KB), served as a
    plain file by Caddy.
  - `clipUrl()` returns the override when the manifest has one, and the
    bundled clip otherwise.
  - If the manifest fails to load, the game plays bundled clips only.
  - `clipUrl()` is synchronous today, with URLs fixed at build time, so the
    manifest has to load before the first clip plays.
- **What the service does:** it's a small Node container. It accepts a take,
  runs ffmpeg to trim, level the volume and encode, and stores the result
  under a content-hash filename. It then rewrites `manifest.json` atomically
  (write to a temp file, then rename). Hash filenames are cached as immutable,
  and only the manifest is revalidated, so a fix shows up on the next load.
- **Takes are kept:** every take stays on disk and the manifest points to the
  active one. That supports reverting to the previous take or to Kokoro, and
  storing per-sound timing marks.
- **Hardening:**
  - Caddy and the service both keep read-only root filesystems.
  - Only the service can write, and only to `./volumes/audio`; Caddy mounts
    that volume read-only.
  - The service runs as non-root with no capabilities and resource limits.
  - It fits the self-hosted Sidecar Caddy → app pattern, and the service
    needs no internet access.
- **Auth:** a Caddy password on `/studio` and `/api` is the simplest start.
  An alternative is the Google sign-in (oauth2-proxy) that portfolio already
  runs.
- **Promote script (optional):** copies the active overrides into the repo as
  new bundled defaults, so a fresh deploy or a lost volume keeps every
  correction.
- **Costs:**
  - Recordings become state, so under self-hosted ADR-0006 the volume needs a
    backup sidecar. Until then, the promote script plus git is the backup.
  - A second container to keep updated (Node plus ffmpeg).
  - The game fetches one small file at start.
- **Rejected:**
  - Caddy WebDAV: needs a custom Caddy build and gives no validation or
    processing.
  - Regenerating Kokoro clips from the studio: would put PyTorch (~1 GB) in
    production, and recording fixes the problem better.
  - Browser-only storage as the main store: fixes stay on one device.
